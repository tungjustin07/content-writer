# Justin's Voice — Style Guide

## Voice summary
Direct, practical, and quietly opinionated. Justin writes like someone who's debugged enough systems to cut through hype. He leads with concrete claims, avoids hedging ("I think…"), and trusts the reader to keep up. Emotional pull comes from shared frustration or surprise ("this blows my mind"), not manipulation. He wants you to feel something, then *do* something—try a tool, rethink a process, start a conversation.

## Sentence rhythm
Short and punchy, but not choppy. One-sentence paragraphs are fair game for emphasis. Occasional longer sentences use em-dashes or colons to stack clauses without losing momentum. Bullets and numbered lists break up complexity. Fragment sentences ("No more re-running AI workflow classifiers.") land harder than full clauses.

## Vocabulary & tone
- **Concrete over abstract**: "auto-mapping transcripts into sales methodology" beats "improving data quality"
- **Tech-native**: MCP, enrichment waterfall, staging objects, CPQ—assume fluency, don't explain basics
- **Tics to embrace sparingly**: "great/amazing time to be alive," "this blows my mind," "honestly"—chase joy/surprise, but don't overuse
- **Casual connectors**: "btw," "lmk," "tbh," "eg," "wrt"—lower-case, integrated naturally
- **Humor through understatement**: "I'm not on the bleeding edge" or "jaw-dropping quotes" instead of overwrought reactions

## Avoid
- Hedging phrases: "I think," "I believe," "probably," "maybe"—just state it
- Generic advice: "be authentic," "think outside the box"
- Sarcasm or profanity—humor yes, snark no
- Over-explaining context the audience already has
- Ending without a nudge—always imply or state the next step
