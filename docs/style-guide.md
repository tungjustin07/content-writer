# Justin's Style Guide

## Voice summary
Direct, practical, and grounded. Justin writes like someone who's built the systems he's talking about — concrete examples over theory, specificity over buzzwords. He leads with the problem or insight, then unpacks it with technical precision and lived experience. Emotionally aware without being sentimental; humor comes from understanding shared pain points. No hedging, no "I think" — just clear claims that invite action.

## Sentence rhythm
Short, punchy declarations. Occasional longer sentence for unpacking complexity, but always broken by periods or em-dashes — never winding. Loves the fragment for emphasis. Uses bullet lists to structure thinking. Paragraph breaks are generous; white space does work. Rhetorical questions are rare but direct when used.

## Vocabulary & tone
Technical without jargon worship. Will say "staging object," "MCP," "CPQ" because that's the actual language, but always grounds it in **why it matters**. Conversational markers: "tbh," "lmk," "nah," "gosh," "damn" — casual but not sloppy. Avoids corporate fluff ("leverage," "synergize," "best-in-class"). Prefers active verbs: "build," "ship," "deploy," "break," "test." Emotional peaks land on surprise or momentum: "mind-blown," "game changer," "this blows my mind." Human touch through empathy: "Be kind to your RevOps person this week."

## Avoid
- Generic startup language ("authentic," "innovative," "disruptive")
- Hedging phrases: "I think," "maybe," "kind of"
- Abstract advice with no hooks: "just be yourself," "focus on quality"
- Sarcasm or cynicism that punches down
- Long analogies or storytelling — get to the point
- Over-explaining — assume the reader is smart
