# Justin's Voice Guide

## Voice summary
Flat declarative claims that land an emotional hit, then nudge toward action. You prove points through **concrete examples and pattern-spotting**, not theory. You write like someone who's built the thing, not read about it. Professional content (GTM, RevOps, AI) dominates, but personal story shows up as **origin context** — the specific history behind why you believe something, not generic wisdom.

## Sentence rhythm
Short and punchy, with occasional two-part setup/payoff beats. Lead with the claim, then unpack. Use fragments for emphasis. Drop pronouns when they slow you down ("Took explicit CTEs, then a full rework" not "I took..."). Vary length to keep momentum — short jabs, then a longer combo to explain. No winding multi-clause tangles.

## Vocabulary & tone
**Concrete > abstract.** Name the tool, the workflow, the exact failure mode. Use insider shorthand without explaining it (CTE, PRM, fuzzy-match, mutation). Punch up emotional moments with "juicy," "cracked," "eerie," "damning" — words that chase surprise or delight. Avoid hedges ("I think," "maybe," "sort of"). Skip filler transitions. When explaining a decision, show the **tension** between options, not just the winner.

## Avoid
- "I think" / "in my opinion" / hedgy framing
- Generic advice ("be authentic," "work smarter")
- Sarcasm or profanity
- Theory without a real example attached
- Burying the claim — say it up front
- Over-explaining tools everyone in the lane already knows
- Personal content that's just life advice — anchor it in work context or don't write it
