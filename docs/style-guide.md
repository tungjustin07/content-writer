# Justin's Writing Style Guide

## Voice summary
Justin writes like a systems architect having coffee—direct, grounded in specifics, and frequently switching between tactical problem-solving and broader pattern recognition. He leads with concrete details (product names, costs, technical specs) before opinions. His style blends engineering precision with GTM operational reality: "I built my own fast mcp" sits comfortably next to "morale is super low wrt many failed implementations." He name-drops liberally not to flex but to establish context and facilitate connections.

## Sentence rhythm
Mix short declarations with longer exploratory sentences. Use fragments for emphasis ("Huge milestone. 🎉"). Deploy parenthetical asides and dashes frequently to add context without breaking flow—like verbal footnotes. Favor simple connectors ("honestly," "btw," "anyways") over formal transitions. Stack questions without preamble when genuinely curious. Lists use bullets or numbered items, often with examples embedded inline via "eg" or "e.g."

## Vocabulary & tone
**Technical without jargon overload**: "vectorized supabase," "mcp," "reverse ETL," "staging object"—but never explaining unless asked
**Casual connectives**: "kinda," "sorta," "tbh," "imo," "wrt," "atm," "ofc"
**Gratitude markers**: "Thanks for bringing me in," "appreciate," "happy to help"
**Qualifiers**: "fairly," "quite," "a bit," "honestly," "I think"
**Action-oriented**: "lmk," "ping me," "come say hi," "let's sync"
**Emoji sparingly**: celebration (🎉🚀), occasional emphasis, never decorative

## Avoid
- Generic advice ("be authentic," "think outside the box")
- Corporate fluff ("excited to announce," "thrilled to share"—unless genuinely launching something)
- Overly polished transitions—prefer abrupt topic shifts with "btw" or "also"
- Lengthy explanations before getting to the point
- Hiding technical complexity behind metaphors
