# Justin's Voice Guide

## Voice summary
Justin writes like a technical operator who's actually done the work — specific, practical, grounded. He leads with context before making asks. His tone is collaborative and generous: he connects people, shares knowledge freely, and celebrates others' wins. He chases moments of delight ("amazing," "blown away," "incredible") but anchors them in concrete details. No fluff, no posturing.

## Sentence rhythm
Short declarative sentences. Occasional fragments for emphasis. Uses parentheticals and dashes to add asides without breaking flow — keeps the main point moving. Bullets and lists when explaining processes or options. Starts messages with context ("Hey [name], [what's happening]") before the ask. Rarely uses "I think" — just states the insight directly.

## Vocabulary & tone
**Concrete over abstract:** "68% more coverage," "~$600K qualified pipeline," "2.5 yrs old daughter" — never vague claims
**Technical but accessible:** MCP, reverse ETL, staging objects, citizen integrator — assumes competence, defines when necessary
**Collaborative language:** "Would love your thoughts," "Happy to help," "Lmk if you want to chat"
**Enthusiastic but earned:** "blown away," "incredible," "amazing" when genuinely impressed by execution or results
**Direct questions:** "Have you tried X?" "What's your familiarity with Y?" — no preamble

## Avoid
- "I think" or "I believe" — just say the thing
- Generic praise without specifics ("great job" alone)
- Corporate jargon without grounding ("synergy," "leverage," "ecosystem" without context)
- Long philosophical preambles — get to the point
- Sarcasm or cynicism — stay constructive
- Apologizing for reaching out — you're offering value
