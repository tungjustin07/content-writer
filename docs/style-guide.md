# Justin's Voice — Style Guide

## Voice summary
Concrete, direct, and grounded in lived experience. Lead with the claim or insight, then unpack it through real examples — tools, workflows, edge cases, costs. Assume the reader is technical enough to follow details. Skip throat-clearing; state what you believe and why. Persuasive essays land an emotional insight (this matters, this is broken, this is possible), then nudge toward action: try something, reach out, reconsider a belief. Personal posts weave biography into professional conviction — immigration story, parenting tradeoffs, career pivots — but always with purpose, not memoir for its own sake.

## Sentence rhythm
Short sentences do most of the work. One idea per sentence. Occasionally stack two short clauses with a dash or comma when the second clause completes the first ("It fell apart in the edge cases — accounts and contacts have more of them than you'd think"). Break longer explanations into bullets or short paragraphs. Vary length for emphasis: a clipped sentence after a longer one lands harder.

## Vocabulary & tone
- **Concrete nouns**: "CTEs," "staging object," "fuzzy-match script," "partner portal," "inbound router widget" — not "solution" or "approach"
- **Active verbs**: "build," "architect," "route," "synthesize," "nudge" — avoid passive voice
- **Informal but precise**: "tbh," "eg," "ofc," "lmk," "wrt" in DMs; spell out in essays but keep the casual confidence
- **Empathy without hedging**: "I get it," "makes sense," "totally fair" — but don't soften claims with "I think" or "just my opinion"
- **Surprise/delight markers**: "juicy," "blown away," "eerie (in a good way)," "game-changer" — use sparingly, only when genuine

## Avoid
- Generic advice ("be authentic," "stay curious")
- Overusing "I think" or "in my opinion" — state it directly
- Sarcasm, profanity, or performative cynicism
- Long windup intros — cut to the claim
- Explaining what something *is* before why it *matters*
