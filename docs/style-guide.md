# Justin's Voice: Style Guide

## Voice summary
Direct, grounded operator who values clarity and speed over polish. Writes like someone solving real problems in real time — short statements, concrete examples, minimal hedging. Prefers emotional resonance over pure logic, but never preachy. Feels like a trusted colleague sharing hard-won lessons, not a thought leader pontificating.

## Sentence rhythm
- **Default to short, declarative statements.** Avoid "I think" or "In my opinion" — just state it.
- **Occasional longer sentence for context or enumeration**, but break it with dashes, commas, or bullets.
- **Use fragments for emphasis.** "Takes courage to pivot." "No profanity, not sarcasm."
- **Lead with the claim, then support.** Don't bury the lede in setup.

## Vocabulary & tone
- **Concrete over abstract.** Say "architect and implement" not "strategize around." Say "data hygiene" not "data quality frameworks."
- **Emotion cues without exclamation spam.** "Amazing," "excited," "proud," "love" — used sparingly but genuinely.
- **Startup/operator shorthand.** TOFU, PQL, ABM, ICP, GTM, SFDC. Assume fluency.
- **Understated humor.** Parenthetical asides, self-aware qualifiers ("might run into throttle issues with these new rules"), gentle ribbing.
- **Empathy hooks.** "Be kind to your RevOps person this week!" Acknowledge complexity.

## Avoid
- **Don't say "I think," "I believe," "In my opinion."** State it or skip it.
- **No sarcasm or profanity.** Humor yes, edge no.
- **No generic inspiration.** Don't write "be authentic" or "think outside the box."
- **No fluff adjectives.** Skip "incredibly," "very," "really" unless they're doing real work.
- **No question framing for rhetorical effect.** Ask real questions or make statements.
